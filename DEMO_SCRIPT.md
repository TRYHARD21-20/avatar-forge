# Avatar Forge — judge demonstration

## Two-minute demo storyboard

- **0:00–0:15 — The hook:** Show the scrapbook-style home page. “Most face datasets feel like chores. What if collecting useful angles was a game?”
- **0:15–0:30 — Informed consent:** Open consent and show mandatory adult/device-only checkboxes with optional research OFF. Start game with research OFF; all modes and rewards remain available.
- **0:30–0:58 — Real angle gate:** On `/capture` first look straight; capture front after hold. Stay straight when LEFT is requested—prove the shot does **not** complete. Then turn smoothly left for ~700ms, receive a shard; repeat right. If a real camera/model fails, show `/dev` timed yaw with the slide and the machine's state readout, label it **synthetic**.
- **0:58–1:20 — Fun over forms:** Play `/solo`, select left/right by looking at choice (or show the head-turn progression). Show result's **Just for fun** marker. Alternatively, invite a second laptop to `/duo` and show answer matching.
- **1:20–1:43 — Rich original art:** Go to Cards; switch Paris → Tokyo → Mumbai. Show the background actually changing with different landmarks. Compose a live portrait, preview Before/After, export 1080×1920. Demonstrate the user-editable illustrated Avatar Forge and four-pose photo-booth strip.
- **1:43–2:00 — Trust:** Show Privacy Dashboard, research submission as a separate click, receipt withdrawal, export/delete and synthetic demo setting. Finish with “All rewards without data contribution.”

## 60-second pitch

**“Face data collection shouldn't feel like giving away your identity for a generic filter.** Avatar Forge turns a consent-based camera flow into a travel photo booth. Players earn angle shards by really turning their head: staying still or tilting doesn't count. They answer fun quizzes, compare answers with a friend, and use locally composited photos to star in fourteen original scenes. The big differentiator is freedom: every reward works when research contribution is OFF. Camera frames stay local until users choose otherwise. A separate encrypted contribution action gives them a signed receipt and a withdrawal path. We’re not claiming mind reading or bank-grade liveness. We’re proving that *better engagement and informed consent* can coexist in one playable prototype.”

## Demo fallback

If network blocks model loading, show `/dev` timed simulator. Say clearly **the simulator does not prove live camera tracking works on this machine**. Don't pretend synthetic yaw is a real user's head pose. You can still demo static artworks and export without camera.
